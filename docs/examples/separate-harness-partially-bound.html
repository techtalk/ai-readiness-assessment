One team, one way of working, four habitats. The same engineers who sit comfortably Coherent in orders-api are working a full level and a half ahead of their environment in legacy-batch. That is not a capability difference — it is an environment difference, and a single-repository assessment of orders-api would have reported a healthy team and missed it.

Portfolio regime

Portfolio regime

Distributed
  • One shared habitat declared for all four subjects.
  • Binds in three; four dimensions inherited-unbound in legacy-batch, whose CI defines its own workflows and whose AGENTS.md restates rather than defers.
  • reporting is bound but pinned 412 days back; it is assessed against the pinned revision.
  • Gap spread 1.79 — driven almost entirely by the unbound subject.

Split ceiling

Common weak — enablement backlog

Observability, Agent composition — weak in three of four, including subjects where the harness does bind. The shared harness does not supply these, so binding harder will not fix them.

Locally weak — team backlogs

legacy-batch: Workflow, Teams provide, Testing, Governance — all four inherited-unbound. A binding failure, not a team failure. It belongs to whoever owns the harness.

What this estate should do next

Bind the harness in legacy-batch. The cheapest uplift in the estate, and not a build — the testing policy and governance model already exist. Four dimensions move the day it lands.

Refresh the reporting pin. It is bound, which is the hard part. It is 412 days behind, which is the easy part.

Then lift observability and agent composition in the harness itself — after the binding work, not before, or you improve a harness half the estate cannot see.

The instinct on seeing this matrix is to treat legacy-batch as a struggling team. The provenance marks say otherwise: the capability exists twenty metres away.

There is no overall score, grade or percentage in this report, and no averaged gap across subjects — averaging destroys the spread, which is the only thing a portfolio view adds. The headline is a distribution and a ceiling.
